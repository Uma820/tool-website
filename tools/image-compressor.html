    What Does the Image Compressor Do?

    The Image Compressor reduces the file size of photographs and graphics without visible degradation in visual clarity. Massive images taken by modern smartphone cameras (often 5 to 12 megabytes each) slow down website loading speeds, exhaust mobile data plans, and exceed email attachment limits.

    By intelligently tuning discrete cosine transform (DCT) quantization tables and offering optional downscaling, this utility strips invisible metadata and optimizes pixel density to achieve 60% to 85% file size reductions entirely inside your browser.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Upload Image: Click Choose Image to select a photo from your computer or phone, or click Sample Image.
    2. Set Compression Level: Drag the quality slider (70%–80% offers the sweet spot between tiny file size and sharp visual quality).
    3. Optional Downscaling: If your photo is a massive 4000px camera shot, choose Max 1920px (Full HD) or Max 1280px to dramatically slash file size further.
    4. Download: View the space savings banner (e.g. 78% reduction) and click Download Compressed.

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    • Compression Engine: Browser-native Canvas 2D image resampling and DCT quantization via toBlob('image/jpeg', quality).
    • Downscaling Algorithm: Bilinear interpolation maintaining exact original aspect ratio.
    • Privacy: Zero remote network transmission. Complete client-side memory execution.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    Lossless PNG images containing solid vectors or screenshots will be converted to JPEG or WebP during compression, which may slightly soften hard text edges. If pixel-perfect graphics or transparency are strictly required, use PNG in the Image Converter.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What compression quality setting is best?

    For most consumer websites and social media, a setting of 75% to 80% reduces file size by over 70% while remaining visually indistinguishable from the uncompressed original.

    Why does resizing save so much space?

    Image file size scales with the total number of pixels. Downscaling a 4000 × 3000 photo (12 megapixels) to 1920 × 1440 (2.7 megapixels) discards over 75% of the raw pixels before compression even begins.

    Are EXIF metadata tags preserved?

    No. When an image is redrawn onto a canvas and compressed, location (GPS) and camera metadata are automatically stripped, which protects your personal privacy and saves extra bytes.

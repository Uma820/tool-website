    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Quilt Backing Calculator computes the exact fabric yardage, panel counts, and cut lengths needed to back any quilt size. By evaluating both vertical (lengthwise grain) and horizontal (crosswise grain) seam orientations side-by-side, it automatically highlights the orientation that minimizes fabric consumption, preventing costly over-purchasing at the fabric store.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Select or enter quilt top dimensions: Choose a preset (such as Throw, Twin, or Queen) or enter custom width and length.
    2. Choose your fabric bolt width: Select standard 42" quilting cotton, 44" apparel cotton, 54" decorator fabric, or 108" seamless wide-back.
    3. Specify overage allowance: Maintain the standard 4" per side (8" total) for longarm quilters, or adjust for domestic pin basting.
    4. Compare vertical vs. horizontal seams: The calculator highlights the winning layout with exact yardage and percentage savings.
    5. Copy your shopping list: Click "Copy Shopping List" to copy formatted cutting details and store yardage to your clipboard.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Backing Dimensions & Panel Logic:

    Backing Width = Quilt Top Width + (2 × Overage per side)
Backing Length = Quilt Top Length + (2 × Overage per side)
Usable Fabric Width = Bolt Width - 2 inches (selvage allowance)
Vertical Panels = ⌈(Backing Width - 2 × Seam Allowance) / (Usable Width - 2 × Seam Allowance)⌉
Total Linear Length = Panels × Cut Length

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    Calculations account for selvage deductions (2 inches total across bolt width) and seam allowance consumption across joined panels. Rounding up to the nearest 1/8 yard reflects standard retail fabric cutting conventions ($1/8\text{ yd} = 4.5\text{ in} = 11.43\text{ cm}$).

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why must fabric selvages be trimmed before piecing quilt backing?

    Selvages are woven with tighter tension than the main fabric body so they do not fray during manufacturing. If left inside a pieced backing seam, they shrink unevenly when laundered, causing puckering along the center of the quilt.

    Should quilt backing seams run vertically or horizontally?

    Both work well! Longarm quilters often prefer vertical seams because they roll parallel to the frame roller bars, ensuring even tension. However, horizontal seams frequently require less total fabric, saving money.

    What needle and thread are recommended for backing seams?

    Use 50wt 100% cotton thread and an 80/12 microtex sharp or universal needle. Press seams open with a dry iron to distribute seam bulk evenly across the batting.

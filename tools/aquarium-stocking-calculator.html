    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Aquarium Stocking Calculator replaces outdated and dangerous "rules of thumb" (such as the notorious "one inch of fish per gallon" rule) with an authentic, biology-based biological load assessment. In aquatic biology, fish waste production scales with body volume and mass (cubic displacement) rather than simple linear length. Furthermore, fish have distinct psychological and territorial requirements: schooling fish suffer chronic stress if kept in groups smaller than 6, fast-swimming species require horizontal swimming length, and coldwater species cannot survive long-term in heated tropical aquariums. This tool computes biological capacity and alerts you to behavioral and water parameter incompatibilities before you purchase fish.

    3. Add Species to Your Tank: Select fish, invertebrates, or amphibians from the 35+ species catalog, choose the desired quantity, and click + Add to Tank.
    4. Inspect Bioload Progress Meter: Check your stocking level gauge:
      • Understocked (<70%): Very easy maintenance, forgiving of skipped water changes.
      • Well-Stocked (70%–95%): Ideal balanced community aquarium.
      • Fully Stocked (95%–105%): Maximum safe biological limit; requires faithful weekly maintenance.
      • Overstocked (>105%): Dangerous risk of sudden ammonia/nitrite spikes and oxygen depletion.
    5. Review Compatibility Alerts: Pay close attention to any warning badges regarding schooling group minimums, fin-nipping risks, or temperature mismatches.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Stocking biological capacity is formulated from surface area gas exchange, water volume, and species-calibrated waste scores:

    • Base Biological Capacity: $$\text{Capacity (Points)} = V_{\text{gal}} \times 2.2 \times \text{Filtration Multiplier}$$
    • Species Bioload Contribution: $$\text{Total Bioload} = \sum (\text{Species Bioload Rating} \times \text{Quantity})$$
    • Stocking Level Percentage: $$\text{Stocking \%} = \frac{\text{Total Bioload}}{\text{Base Capacity}} \times 100$$
    • Recommended Weekly Water Change:
      • $\le 70\%\text{ Stocked}$: $15\%–20\%$ weekly
      • $70\%–95\%\text{ Stocked}$: $25\%$ weekly
      • $95\%–105\%\text{ Stocked}$: $35\%$ weekly
      • $> 105\%\text{ Stocked}$: $50\%+\text{ weekly}$

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why do my fish stay at the bottom or hide constantly?

    Schooling species (such as tetras, rasboras, and corydoras) kept in groups smaller than their social minimum (6+) perceive open water as unsafe. Adding more individuals of the same species and providing dense live plant cover gives them the collective confidence to swim openly.

    How does adding live plants affect my stocking capacity?

    Heavily planted aquariums have substantially higher biological capacity. Fast-growing aquatic stem plants (such as Hornwort, Anacharis, and Water Wisteria) directly consume toxic ammonia and nitrate as fertilizer, acting as a living supplemental biological filter. Selecting "Heavily Planted + Canister" in our calculator grants a 40% boost to biological capacity.

    Can I add all my fish at the same time?

    No, never add an entire stocking list at once to a newly cycled tank. Adding too many fish simultaneously causes an ammonia spike that overwhelms your biological filter bacteria. Always add fish in small batches of 4 to 6 individuals spaced 1 to 2 weeks apart, testing water parameters regularly.

    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Bread Recipe Scaler eliminates guesswork and error when adjusting bread, sourdough, pizza, and pastry batches. Whether scaling up a 1-loaf artisan boule to bake 4 loaves for friends, halving an enriched brioche formula, or adjusting a recipe to fit exactly 650g of flour remaining in your pantry, this tool recalculates every ingredient with culinary precision.

    Standard home kitchen scaling often suffers from two critical errors: rough fraction approximations and unbalanced leavening. Unlike general cooking where adding a pinch more liquid or salt has negligible impact, baking is strict biochemical stoichiometry. By scaling by exact percentages and applying smart decimal rounding (0.1g for micro-ingredients like yeast and salt, 1g for bulk flour and water), your dough hydration, crumb structure, and fermentation rates stay perfectly preserved.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Recipe scaling operates through proportional linear transformation anchored by a global scale factor ($S$):

    • Multiplier Scaling:
      $$S = M$$ where $M$ is the explicit user multiplier factor.
    • Yield Count Scaling:
      $$S = \frac{Y_{\text{target}}}{Y_{\text{original}}}$$ where $Y$ is the number of loaves, buns, or baking pans.
    • Available Flour Scaling:
      $$S = \frac{W_{\text{flour, available}}}{\sum W_{\text{flour, original}}}$$ where $W$ represents weight in grams.
    • Scaled Ingredient Weight ($W_{\text{scaled}}$):
      $$W_{\text{scaled}} = W_{\text{original}} \times S$$
    • Smart Precision Rounding:
      For micro-ingredients ($W_{\text{scaled}} < 25\text{g}$), values are preserved to $0.1\text{g}$ resolution ($round(W \times 10) / 10$). For bulk ingredients ($\ge 25\text{g}$), integers ($1\text{g}$) are enforced to prevent impractical kitchen fractions.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    While ingredient weight scales linearly, fermentation dynamics do not scale completely 1:1 at massive commercial volumes (e.g., 50kg+ dough mixers). Large masses of fermenting dough generate significant metabolic friction heat and retain internal warmth longer than a small 1kg home bowl, which may slightly accelerate proofing times. For standard home and cottage scale (up to 10 loaves), linear scaling remains completely accurate.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How does this tool scale yeast when doubling or tripling a recipe?

    By default, this tool scales yeast proportionally with flour. While some commercial high-volume bakeries reduce yeast percentage slightly for 50kg batches due to dough heat buildup, for home and cottage batch sizes (1 to 10 loaves) proportional scaling maintains consistent fermentation timing.

    Can I add non-flour ingredients like butter, milk, or seeds?

    Yes! Simply tap the + Add Ingredient button, type the ingredient name, and leave the "Is Flour" box unchecked. The scaler will scale them in exact proportion to your recipe.

    What if my original recipe is listed in cups and tablespoons?

    For best results in bread making, convert volume measurements to weight before scaling, as 1 cup of flour can vary from 120g to 160g depending on humidity and packing. Once entered by weight, scaling is mathematically flawless.

    Does the tool remember my checked ingredients if I toggle modes?

    Yes. Your checklist completion statuses are stored in memory during your active session so you won't lose your place while baking.

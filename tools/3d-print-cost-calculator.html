    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The 3D Print Cost Calculator calculates the complete, authentic cost of manufacturing 3D printed objects. Most makers and print farm owners make the fatal business mistake of only calculating filament weight (e.g. "This model used 50g of PLA, so it cost me $1.00"). In reality, filament represents only 20% to 35% of true manufacturing overhead.

    By accounting for machine depreciation, maintenance wear (nozzles, belts, PEI sheets), utility electricity consumption, labor time (slicing, file prep, post-processing), and an inevitable failure buffer, this tool reveals your genuine break-even cost and calculates competitive retail prices for Etsy, Shopify, or commercial client quotes.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Choose Currency & Batch Quantity: Select your preferred currency ($ USD, ₹ INR, € EUR, or £ GBP) and enter how many identical prints are in the batch order.
    2. Add Material / Filament Rows: Input the grams of filament used (from your slicer preview) and your spool purchase price. For multi-material prints (e.g. Bambu AMS or Prusa MMU), tap + Add Filament to add extra color rows.
    3. Set Print Time & Wattage: Enter the slicer's estimated print time in hours and minutes. Choose your printer model preset (Bambu Lab, Ender 3, Prusa, Voron) to load realistic average power consumption.
    4. Configure Machine Depreciation & Labor: Enter prep and support cleanup minutes, your hourly rate, and the printer's purchase price to amortize hardware costs.
    5. Adjust Markup Multiplier: Pick a markup preset (1.5x for friends, 2.5x standard, 3.0x Etsy, 4.0x commercial) to establish your retail price and net profit margin.
    6. Copy Customer Quote: Tap Copy Customer Quote to copy a structured text quote ready for emails or invoices.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    True production cost integrates five distinct accounting vectors:

    • Material Cost ($C_{\text{material}}$):
      $$C_{\text{material}} = \sum \left( W_{\text{used}} \times \frac{\text{Spool Cost}}{W_{\text{spool}}} \right)$$
    • Electricity Cost ($C_{\text{elec}}$):
      $$C_{\text{elec}} = \frac{P_{\text{watts}} \times T_{\text{hours}}}{1000} \times R_{\text{kWh}}$$
    • Machine Depreciation & Maintenance ($C_{\text{machine}}$):
      $$C_{\text{machine}} = \left( \frac{\text{Printer Cost}}{L_{\text{lifespan hours}}} + M_{\text{maint per hour}} \right) \times T_{\text{hours}}$$
    • Labor Cost ($C_{\text{labor}}$):
      $$C_{\text{labor}} = \frac{\text{Labor Minutes}}{60} \times R_{\text{hourly}}$$
    • Failure Buffer & Total True Production Cost ($C_{\text{total}}$):
      $$\text{Subtotal} = C_{\text{material}} + C_{\text{elec}} + C_{\text{machine}} + C_{\text{labor}}$$ $$C_{\text{total}} = \text{Subtotal} \times \left( 1 + \frac{\text{Buffer \%}}{100} \right)$$
    • Retail Price & Profit Margin:
      $$\text{Selling Price} = C_{\text{total}} \times \text{Markup Multiplier}$$ $$\text{Profit Margin \%} = \frac{\text{Selling Price} - C_{\text{total}}}{\text{Selling Price}} \times 100\%$$

    Realistic Example & Expected Output

    Scenario: Printing an articulated dragon that uses 120g of Silk PLA ($24/kg), runs for 6 hours on a Bambu P1S, and requires 15 minutes of packaging.

    • Material: $120\text{g} \times \$0.024/\text{g} = \$2.88$
    • Electricity: $110\text{W} \times 6\text{h} / 1000 = 0.66\text{ kWh} \times \$0.15 = \$0.10$
    • Machine Depreciation: $(\$600 / 3000\text{h} + \$0.10) \times 6\text{h} = \$0.30 \times 6 = \$1.80$
    • Labor: $0.25\text{h} \times \$20/\text{h} = \$5.00$
    • Failure Buffer (10%): $(\$2.88 + \$0.10 + \$1.80 + \$5.00) \times 0.10 = \$0.98$
    • Total True Production Cost: $\$10.76$
    • Recommended Selling Price (2.5x): $\$26.90$ ($\$16.14$ net profit per unit).

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Should I charge clients for failed prints?

    Never bill clients directly for failed prints; doing so creates friction. Instead, maintain a 10% to 15% failure rate buffer in your base production cost. This organically covers scrapped filament and printer downtime across your overall pricing structure.

    How do I account for multi-color purge waste?

    When multi-color 3D printing (e.g. Bambu AMS), enter your total filament consumed (model weight + purge waste + prime tower weight) into the material weight field, or use our dedicated Multi-Color Waste Calculator.

    What hourly labor rate should a 3D printing business charge?

    For basic slicing and packing, $15 to $25/hour is common. For CAD modeling, organic mesh repair, and technical engineering advice, standard billable rates range from $50 to $100+/hour.

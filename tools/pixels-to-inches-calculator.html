    What Is Bleed Allowance and When Do I Need It?

    Bleed is extra artwork that extends 1/8" (0.125") or 1/4" (0.25") beyond the final trim edge of your print. When a printer cuts stacks of paper, the blade can shift slightly — bleed ensures your design fills the page edge-to-edge with no white gaps.

    • When you need bleed: Commercial printing, POD products (Printful, Printify, Redbubble), greeting cards, flyers, business cards, and any design with a full-bleed background.
    • When you don't need it: Digital-only products (PDF downloads, Etsy digital art files), screen mockups, or when the printer specifically says no bleed required.
    • Standard bleed: 1/8" (0.125") per side = 0.25" total added to each dimension. At 300 DPI that's 75 pixels per side.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    This calculator computes exact geometric pixel and physical dimensions based on mathematical conversions. It does not inspect individual raster image files or evaluate sub-pixel optical sharpness. Actual perceived print sharpness also depends on the physical printer technology (halftone screens, inkjet droplet dispersion) and the viewing distance of the finished printed artwork.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I convert pixels to inches?

    Divide the pixel dimension by the DPI. Example: 3000 pixels at 300 DPI = 3000 ÷ 300 = 10.00 inches. Use the formula Inches = Pixels ÷ DPI.

    What DPI should I use for Printful or Printify?

    Printful and Printify both recommend 300 DPI for the sharpest results. The minimum they accept is typically 150 DPI, but anything below 300 may appear pixelated or blurry, especially on close-inspection products like mugs and phone cases.

    What pixel size is 8×10 at 300 DPI?

    An 8×10 inch print at 300 DPI requires a canvas of 2400×3000 pixels. With standard 1/8" bleed on all sides, the canvas becomes 2475×3075 pixels. With 1/4" bleed, it becomes 2550×3150 pixels.

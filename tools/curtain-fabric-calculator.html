    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Curtain Fabric Calculator calculates the total linear yardage, fabric widths (drops), and seam joining specifications required for custom curtains and drapery. It factors in curtain track or pole width, heading fullness styles, side hems, top header tape, bottom weighted hems, and vertical pattern repeat matching.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Measure your curtain pole or track: Enter the full rod width across the window (not just the window glass width).
    2. Measure the finished drop: Measure from the top of your pole or track down to your desired sill or floor position.
    3. Select panel setup: Choose Pair of Curtains (2 Panels) or Single Panel.
    4. Select heading style & fullness: Choose standard pencil pleat (2.0x), sheer/wave (2.5x), grommet/eyelet (1.8x), or tailored pinch pleat (1.5x).
    5. Specify fabric width & pattern repeat: Select 54" drapery standard or enter custom width. If patterned, enter the vertical repeat.
    6. Copy shopping list: Click "Copy Shopping List" to copy panel cutting instructions and yardage to your clipboard.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Drapery Fullness & Cut Drop Calculations:

    Total Flat Width = (Rod Width × Fullness Multiplier) + (Side Hems × Number of Panels)
Widths Needed = ⌈Total Flat Width / Fabric Bolt Width⌉
Unadjusted Cut Drop = Finished Drop + Header Allowance + Bottom Hem
Cut Drop with Repeat = ⌈Unadjusted Cut Drop / Vertical Repeat⌉ × Vertical Repeat
Total Linear Fabric = Widths Needed × Cut Drop
Total Yardage = Total Linear Inches / 36

    Common Practical Use Cases

    • Living Room Floor-Length Drapery: Calculating yardage across large 72" or 96" sliding doors and picture windows.
    • Sheer Voile Wave Curtains: Sizing high-fullness (2.5x) sheer curtains for daylight diffusion and privacy.
    • Odd-Width Curtain Splits: Determining how to split an odd number of widths (such as 3 widths into two 1.5-width panels) and conceal seams against the outer wall.

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    Calculations include a default 14-inch total hem allowance (4 inches for top buckram heading plus 8 to 10 inches for a double-turned weighted bottom hem). If a vertical pattern repeat is entered, every drop length is automatically rounded up to the next integer multiple of the pattern repeat to guarantee horizontal motif alignment across seams.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    Calculations assume standard straight poles or flat tracks. For angled bay windows or curved bow tracks, calculate each track section independently to determine panel widths per window segment.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What if my calculation calls for 3 widths on a pair of curtains?

    When a pair of curtains requires an odd number of widths (such as 3 widths total), each curtain panel gets 1.5 widths. Cut one full fabric width in half lengthwise down the center. Join each half-width to the outer edge of a full width so the seam is hidden against the wall when drawn.

    Should curtains touch the floor or hover above it?

    Floor-length curtains typically hover 1/2 inch (1.25 cm) above the floor so they clear dust and slide smoothly. For a romantic or luxurious look, "pooling" or "puddling" adds 2 to 4 inches of extra drop to rest elegantly on the floor.

    Should I use curtain lining?

    Yes. Lining protects face fabric from UV sun damage, improves thermal insulation, and adds body to help folds hang uniformly. Simply calculate identical yardage for your lining fabric.

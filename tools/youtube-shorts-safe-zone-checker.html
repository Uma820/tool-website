What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

YouTube Shorts has rapidly expanded into one of the largest video distribution channels on the web, receiving billions of daily views. However, the YouTube Shorts mobile player layers a prominent suite of native buttons—including the bright red Subscribe badge, channel avatar, like/dislike counts, and sound remix buttons—over your 9:16 footage.

Placing subtitles, lower thirds, or key instructional text in the bottom 380 pixels or along the right 180 pixels causes them to clash with these clickable icons. This dedicated YouTube Shorts Safe-Zone Previewer lets you test your footage against authentic mobile and desktop Shorts player overlays and export high-resolution 1080×1920 transparent PNG guides for your video editing workflow.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Upload Video or Image: Drag your 9:16 MP4, MOV, or WebM video into the drop zone, or click "Load 9:16 Demo Frame" to explore the player.
  2. Test Player Modes: Toggle between Mobile Full-Screen Player and Desktop Theater Player to see how your content renders across devices.
  3. Customize Your Title: Type your actual YouTube Shorts video title to check whether your text wraps into two or three lines and collides with lower subtitles.
  4. Inspect CAD Danger Zones: Switch to the Clean Safe Zone Grid to review exact pixel margins and verify that faces, titles, and product shots sit inside the green box.
  5. Download PNG Overlay: Click "Download Shorts 1080×1920 PNG Overlay" to import the transparent template directly into CapCut, Premiere Pro, or DaVinci Resolve.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the red Subscribe button so important to avoid?

The Subscribe button on YouTube Shorts is bright red, high contrast, and interactive. If your video's subtitles or punchline land behind it, viewers' eyes are drawn to the button rather than your text, harming retention and watch time.

Can I upload a 4K vertical video (2160×3840)?

Yes. High-resolution 4K clips are scaled proportionately to 1080×1920 inside the browser canvas while preserving exact safe zone margins.

How do I avoid having YouTube Shorts cover my video progress?

YouTube renders a thin red playback progress bar along the very bottom pixel edge. Keeping captions at least 380px above the bottom edge guarantees zero overlap with both the progress bar and description box.

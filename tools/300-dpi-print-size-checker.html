    What This Tool Does

    The 300 DPI POD Product Readiness Checker analyzes your artwork's pixel dimensions and calculates the effective print resolution (DPI) across every product in a database of 20+ print-on-demand items. Rather than guessing whether your 4500×5400px design will look sharp on a poster, mug, or T-shirt, this tool instantly gives you a color-coded badge for each product — ✅ Crisp (300+ DPI), ⚠️ Acceptable (180–299 DPI), or ❌ Too Low (<180 DPI).

    Understanding DPI: Crisp vs Acceptable vs Blurry

    DPI (dots per inch) describes how many pixels of your artwork map onto each physical inch of the printed product. Higher DPI = more detail per inch = sharper print.

    • ✅ 300+ DPI — Crisp: Industry standard for professional print quality. All fine details, thin lines, and text render sharply at typical viewing distances. Required for phone cases and small accessories.
    • ⚠️ 180–299 DPI — Acceptable: Will look decent for large-format products (big posters, oversized canvases) or items viewed from a distance (e.g. 24×36 poster on a wall). Not ideal for detailed artwork or text.
    • ❌ Below 180 DPI — Too Low: The image will appear visibly blurry, pixelated, or "jaggy" on the printed product. Many platforms will reject artwork below their minimum threshold (typically 150 DPI) automatically.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What image size does Merch by Amazon require?

    Merch by Amazon requires a minimum of 4500 × 5400 pixels at 300 DPI for adult standard T-shirts. The image must be a transparent PNG under 25 MB. For youth tees (smaller print area), lower resolutions may pass but 300 DPI is always recommended. MBA automatically rejects uploads that fall below their quality threshold.

    Can I use the same artwork file for all POD products?

    Possibly — if your artwork is high resolution (5400+ px on the long edge) and roughly square, it can cover most apparel, mid-size posters, and drinkware at 300 DPI. However, wide products like mugs (9:3.75 ratio) need horizontal artwork, while tall products like phone cases need portrait orientation. Use the aspect ratio mismatch warnings in this tool to identify where your image will be auto-cropped.

    Is 150 DPI good enough for a large poster?

    For large-format posters (18"×24" and bigger), 150 DPI is generally acceptable because viewers stand several feet away from the print. At typical viewing distance, the human eye cannot distinguish individual dots at 150 DPI on a poster. For small posters (8"×10", 11"×14") and phone cases viewed close-up, always aim for 300 DPI to maintain sharp, professional quality.

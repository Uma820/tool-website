    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Chargeable Weight Calculator resolves the critical pricing disparity between actual weight and dimensional volume. In commercial air and ocean shipping, transport carriers do not bill strictly by scale weight; instead, they bill by Chargeable Weight. This tool automatically compares your shipment's gross deadweight against its volumetric space (applying international IATA 0.5 kg rounding for air freight, or the 1 CBM : 1,000 kg W/M standard for ocean LCL), identifying your exact billing basis and cost-driver.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Select shipment mode: Toggle between Air / Express Courier and Sea Freight (LCL W/M).
    2. Select your unit preference: Switch between Metric ($cm / kg$) or Imperial ($in / lbs$).
    3. Configure DIM divisor: For air cargo, standard IATA utilizes divisor $6,000$. For express couriers like DHL, FedEx, or UPS, select divisor $5,000$.
    4. Enter box rows: Input box length, width, height, gross weight per piece, and quantities.
    5. Evaluate the billing decision: Check the badge indicator to see whether your consignment is penalized for being bulky (Volumetric) or dense (Actual Weight).

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Air Freight Chargeable Weight Rule:

    Chargeable Weight = Max(Total Actual Gross Weight, Total Volumetric Weight)
Carrier Billed Weight = Ceiling(Chargeable Weight × 2) / 2  (Rounded up to next 0.5 kg)

    Ocean Freight (LCL W/M Revenue Tons) Rule:

    Metric Tons = Gross Weight (kg) / 1,000
Revenue Tons (W/M) = Max(Total CBM, Metric Tons)  [Minimum: 1.0 W/M]

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How does the 0.5 kg carrier rounding work?

    Airlines and express couriers do not bill fractional weights below 0.5 kg. For instance, if your shipment has an exact chargeable weight of 12.1 kg, you will be billed for 12.5 kg. If it weighs 12.51 kg, it rounds up to 13.0 kg.

    What does 'Dense Cargo' vs 'Bulky Cargo' mean?

    Dense cargo (such as steel, liquids, or machinery) has high weight in a small space, meaning it is billed by Actual Weight. Bulky cargo (such as shoes, apparel, or empty containers) takes up lots of aircraft space and is billed by Volumetric Weight.

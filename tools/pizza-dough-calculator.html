    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Pizza Dough Calculator computes the exact gram or ounce measurements for homemade and commercial pizza dough batches. Instead of guessing flour amounts or struggling with messy fractions, it uses exact total dough weight back-calculation: you simply choose your pizza style, number of dough balls, and target weight, and the calculator mathematically back-calculates the exact flour baseline ($100\%$) and balances all water, salt, yeast, and fat ratios.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Select your pizza style preset: Choose Neapolitan (62%) for high-heat wood ovens, New York Style (63%) for conventional home ovens, or Detroit (70%) for crispy, thick pan crusts.
    2. Specify dough balls and size: Enter how many pizzas you plan to bake and choose your desired ball weight (e.g. 250g for 12" pies).
    3. Choose your yeast type: Select Instant Dry Yeast (IDY), Active Dry Yeast (ADY), Fresh Cake Yeast, or Sourdough Starter. The calculator automatically adjusts yeast percentages.
    4. Fine-tune percentages (optional): Adjust hydration, salt, or oil if desired. The ingredients update instantaneously in real time.
    5. Copy your recipe formula: Click "Copy Recipe Formula" to copy formatted gram measurements directly to your notes or kitchen board.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Total Batch Back-Calculation Equations:

    Total Dough Weight = Number of Balls × Target Ball Weight
Total Baker's % = 100% (Flour) + Hydration% + Salt% + Yeast% + Oil% + Sugar%
Flour Weight (g) = Total Dough Weight / (Total Baker's % / 100)
Water Weight (g) = Flour Weight × (Hydration% / 100)
Salt Weight (g) = Flour Weight × (Salt% / 100)
Yeast Weight (g) = Flour Weight × (Yeast% / 100)

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    The algorithm uses double-precision floating point arithmetic. Flour and water weights round to the nearest whole integer gram ($1\text{g}$), while fine ingredients like salt, instant yeast, and oil are rounded to tenths of a gram ($0.1\text{g}$) for high-precision digital kitchen scales.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    Ambient room temperature and dough fermentation times (e.g. 2-hour warm ferment vs. 72-hour cold retard) influence the final proofing activity of the yeast. In warmer climates ($>26^\circ\text{C} / 78^\circ\text{F}$), reduce yeast percentages by 20% to 30% to prevent over-fermentation.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why does my pizza dough tear when stretching?

    Dough tearing is usually caused by under-developed gluten, cold dough (always let refrigerated dough balls warm up for 1.5 to 2 hours at room temperature before opening), or insufficient dough relaxation after balling.

    Can I use all-purpose flour instead of Tipo 00 or Bread flour?

    Yes, but unbleached Bread Flour (12.5% to 14% protein) or Italian Tipo 00 flour is strongly recommended. Higher protein flour develops the gluten network required to trap expanding steam and ferment gases, yielding an airy cornicione.

    How long should pizza dough ferment?

    A 24 to 72 hour cold fermentation in the refrigerator develops deep aroma, organic acids, and micro-bubbles in the crust while breaking down complex carbohydrates for easier digestion.

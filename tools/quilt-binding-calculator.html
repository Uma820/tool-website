    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Quilt Binding Calculator computes the exact fabric requirements for edging your finished quilts. It accurately calculates the quilt perimeter, adds corner and diagonal joining allowances, and determines the exact number of strips needed for standard Straight-Grain (WOF) cutting or the fabric square size for Continuous Bias binding.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Select a quilt size or enter dimensions: Choose from standard presets (Crib, Throw, Twin, Queen, King) or enter custom width and length.
    2. Select binding strip width: Choose 2.5" (most popular for French double-fold binding), 2.25", 2.0", or enter a custom width.
    3. Confirm fabric bolt width: Enter your fabric width (default 42" for standard quilting cotton).
    4. Choose cutting method: Compare Method A (Straight-Grain WOF) for rectangular straight-edge quilts versus Method B (Continuous Bias) for curved edges.
    5. Copy shopping list: Click "Copy Shopping List" to copy exact strip counts and yardage directly to your clipboard.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Perimeter & WOF Strip Calculations:

    Perimeter = 2 × (Quilt Width + Quilt Length)
Total Binding Length = Perimeter + 12" (corner & joining allowance)
Usable WOF = Bolt Width - 2" (selvage allowance)
Strips to Cut = ⌈Total Binding Length / Usable WOF⌉
Fabric Yardage = (Strips × Strip Width) / 36

    Continuous Bias Square Formula:

    Bias Binding Area = Total Binding Length × Strip Width
Square Side Required = ⌈√(Bias Binding Area) + 2"⌉

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    Straight-grain strip calculations use an effective usable width of $\text{Bolt Width} - 2''$ to ensure dense, fray-resistant selvages are never incorporated into the binding. All fractional yardage estimates round up to the nearest $1/8$ yard ($4.5\text{ in}$) to ensure safety at retail fabric counters.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    Calculations assume standard double-fold French binding attached with 1/4" seam allowance. For extra-thick high-loft polyester battings, increase strip width to 2.75" or 3" to accommodate edge wrap without pulling the quilt border.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why join binding strips at a 45-degree angle instead of a straight seam?

    Joining strips at a 45-degree angle distributes seam allowance bulk across a longer distance along the quilt edge. A straight 90-degree seam stacks multiple fabric layers in one spot, creating a thick ridge that sewing machine needles struggle to pass through.

    What is the difference between single-fold and double-fold binding?

    Double-fold (French) binding is folded in half lengthwise with wrong sides together before stitching, giving two layers of protective fabric over the edge for durability. Single-fold binding is only one layer and is usually reserved for decorative placemats or apparel.

    Can I use leftover backing fabric for my binding?

    Yes! Self-binding with leftover backing fabric creates a clean, coordinated look. If you cut extra lengthwise or horizontal panels from your backing, you can often cut all your binding strips from the trimmed remnants.

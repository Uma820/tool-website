    What Does the YouTube Money Calculator Do?

    The YouTube Money & Earnings Calculator helps video creators, aspiring YouTubers, and digital marketers forecast potential ad revenue from their channels. Video monetization is governed by RPM (Revenue Per Mille), representing the amount of ad revenue a creator earns per 1,000 video views after YouTube's platform cut.

    By adjusting estimated daily view volumes and category RPM rates, creators can visualize realistic daily, monthly, and annual revenue goals, plan upload schedules, and evaluate the earning potential of different content niches.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Set Daily Views: Move the views slider or type your daily average view count into the box.
    2. Select or Adjust RPM: Pick a preset niche (such as Gaming, Tech, or Finance) or manually adjust the RPM slider to match your channel's actual YouTube Analytics dashboard.
    3. Review Projections: Instantly inspect projected daily, monthly, and annual AdSense earnings.
    4. Model Growth Scenarios: Test higher view counts to evaluate how publishing more videos or increasing view duration could impact your monthly income.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the difference between CPM and RPM?

    CPM (Cost Per Mille) is what advertisers pay YouTube for 1,000 ad impressions. RPM (Revenue Per Mille) is the actual metric that matters to creators: it measures how much money the creator earns per 1,000 total video views after YouTube's 45% revenue cut.

    Why do different niches have different RPMs?

    Advertisers bid higher rates on audiences with high purchasing intent. Financial software, real estate, and B2B SaaS companies compete fiercely for adult viewers interested in investments, resulting in $10–$20+ RPMs. In contrast, gaming or comedy channels attract younger viewers where general advertisers bid lower rates ($1.50–$3.00 RPM).

    Do YouTube Shorts pay the same as long-form videos?

    No. YouTube Shorts currently have significantly lower RPMs (typically $0.05 to $0.20 per 1,000 views) because ads run between Shorts in the feed rather than directly on individual videos.

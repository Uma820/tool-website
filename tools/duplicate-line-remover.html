    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Duplicate Line Remover identifies and purges duplicate lines from any list, preserving the first appearance of every unique line. It solves the problem of bloated lists, duplicate marketing contacts, repeated keywords, and redundant server entries.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Paste your list into the text area.
    2. Configure options: toggle "Case-Sensitive Matching" or "Trim Leading/Trailing Spaces".
    3. Click "Remove Duplicates" to analyze the lines.
    4. Inspect the statistics (total lines, unique lines, duplicates removed) and copy your deduplicated list.

    Common Practical Use Cases

    • Email & Subscriber Deduplication: Prevent sending multiple marketing emails to the same recipient by removing duplicate email addresses.
    • Search Engine Keyword Optimization: Clean up large keyword research sheets by eliminating redundant search queries.
    • Inventory & SKU Manifests: Verify that every item SKU or serial number appears exactly once in an inventory file.

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    Operates using a JavaScript Set hash structure with $O(N)$ time complexity. Case-insensitive mode normalizes keys via .toLowerCase() while preserving the original casing of the first-seen line in the final output.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    Preserves the first instance seen and discards subsequent identical rows. To sort the remaining lines alphabetically, use the Text Sorter tool.

    Privacy & Security Guarantee

    Processes your text entirely in client-side RAM. No logs, cookies, or external requests.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Is case-sensitive matching supported?

    Yes. You can toggle case sensitivity on or off depending on whether "Apple" and "apple" should be treated as identical.

    Does this tool preserve the original line order?

    Yes. The first occurrence of each unique line remains in its original position.

    Can it handle lists with tens of thousands of lines?

    Yes. The hash set algorithm processes tens of thousands of rows in milliseconds on modern browsers.

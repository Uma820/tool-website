What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

Instagram Reels has a unique dual-display challenge that trips up even experienced video creators: full-screen 9:16 playback versus profile feed grid cropping. When viewers watch your Reel in the Reels feed, they see the full 1080×1920 vertical canvas. But when they visit your profile grid, Instagram automatically crops the video to a centered 4:5 ratio (1080×1350) or a square 1:1 ratio (1080×1080).

If your thumbnail hook or title is placed too high or too low, the top and bottom 285 pixels are sliced off, leaving your profile grid looking untidy and cutting off vital context. This tool gives you simultaneous visibility over both the full-screen Reels HUD and the profile grid center crop.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Upload Video or Cover Art: Drop your 9:16 MP4/MOV video or PNG/JPG image into the drop zone, or click "Load 9:16 Demo Frame".
  2. Inspect the 4:5 Profile Grid Crop: Look at the dashed yellow 4:5 rectangle. Ensure all thumbnail text, titles, and faces fit comfortably between Y: 285px and Y: 1635px.
  3. Check Full-Screen Reels Safe Zone: Check the green safe boundary to verify that speech subtitles avoid the bottom username and right-side heart/comment rail.
  4. Test Custom Captions: Type your actual Instagram caption in the text box to test how many lines it occupies over your video footage.
  5. Export Transparent Template: Click "Download Reels + 4:5 Grid PNG Overlay" to import a 1080×1920 guide layer directly into CapCut, Premiere Pro, or Canva.

How to Use Safe-Zone Templates in CapCut, Premiere Pro & DaVinci

Using the downloaded 1080×1920 transparent PNG guide in video editors:

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did Instagram switch profile covers to 4:5?

Instagram updated profile grids to prioritize vertical content, shifting from square 1:1 thumbnails to 4:5 (1080×1350) aspect ratios. Keeping key cover art inside the 4:5 box ensures your grid looks modern, professional, and visually centered.

Can I choose a custom cover frame using this tool?

Yes! When you upload a video, you can pause at any exact frame and inspect how that specific moment will look on both full-screen Reels and the 4:5 profile grid before deciding on your cover frame.

Where should I put my subtitles on Instagram Reels?

Place speech subtitles in the center vertical third of the frame (between Y: 800px and Y: 1350px). This keeps them safe from the bottom username and caption bar, and keeps them readable when shared to the home feed.

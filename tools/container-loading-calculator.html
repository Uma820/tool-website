    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Container Loading Calculator evaluates whether your export cargo will fit inside standard intermodal ocean containers (20ft, 40ft, and 40ft High Cube). Beyond raw volume, it cross-references cargo gross weight against container maximum structural payloads and checks for oversized box dimensions that cannot clear container door frames. It guides supply chain planners on equipment selection and flags whether your shipment will volume-out or weight-out first.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Set the packing efficiency buffer: The slider defaults to $85\%$ to model real-world broken stowage (adjust between $70\%$ for irregular cartons and $95\%$ for uniform interlocking cartons).
    2. Indicate stackability: Check or uncheck "Cartons Stackable" based on whether your cargo packaging is crush-sensitive.
    3. Select your unit preference: Switch between Metric ($cm / kg$) or Imperial ($in / lbs$).
    4. Enter box inventory: Fill in lengths, widths, heights, unit gross weights, and carton counts.
    5. Review the container stuffing recommendation: The tool suggests whether a 20'GP, 40'GP, or 40'HC is best suited, and highlights any container payload or door threshold violations.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Usable Container Volume & Space Utilization:

    Usable Volume (m³) = Nominal Container CBM × (Efficiency % / 100)
Volume Utilization % = (Total Cargo CBM / Usable Volume) × 100
Weight Payload Utilization % = (Total Cargo Weight kg / Max Container Payload kg) × 100

    • Container Stuffing Optimization: Ensuring factory shipments utilize maximum container capacity to lower per-unit freight costs.

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    Internal container dimensions adhere to ISO 668 specifications. Payload ratings reflect standard heavy-tested CSC plate ratings. Warning algorithms trap cartons exceeding container internal width ($235\text{ cm}$) or door height ($228\text{ cm}$ for GP, $258\text{ cm}$ for HC).

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    While this tool computes realistic volumetric packing with broken stowage buffers, specialized cargo (e.g., long steel pipes or machinery with non-uniform centers of gravity) requires professional 3D load planning software and axle-weight distribution testing.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why is the maximum weight payload lower on a 40ft container than a 20ft container?

    A 40ft container has a larger steel tare weight (approx. 3,750 kg vs. 2,300 kg for a 20ft). Because total gross weight (container + cargo) is regulated to roughly 30,480 kg, the 40ft container has slightly less cargo payload capacity than a 20ft container.

    What does High Cube (HC) offer over standard 40ft containers?

    High Cube containers provide an extra 30 cm (1 foot) of vertical clearance ($269\text{ cm}$ vs $239\text{ cm}$). This provides approximately $8.7\text{ CBM}$ more volume, making it the preferred choice for lightweight, bulky goods like furniture and apparel.

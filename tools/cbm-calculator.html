    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The CBM Calculator computes the exact cubic meter volume ($\text{m}^3$), cubic feet ($\text{ft}^3$), and gross weight for international freight shipments. Whether you are shipping commercial cartons via ocean container (FCL / LCL), air freight, or road transit, calculating your total CBM is the fundamental first step required by freight forwarders, shipping lines, and customs brokers to quote shipping rates.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Select your unit of measurement: Toggle between Metric (cm / kg) or Imperial (in / lbs) to match your supplier's packing list.
    2. Enter carton dimensions: Input the Length, Width, and Height of your shipping boxes along with gross unit weight and quantity.
    3. Add multiple box sizes: If your consignment contains different carton sizes, click "+ Add Another Item" to create additional rows.
    4. Review the summary: Total CBM, Cubic Feet, Gross Weight, and estimated 20ft/40ft container fill rates update in real time.
    5. Copy or transfer data: Click "Copy Results" to paste the manifest into an email or quote request, or click "Send to Container" to simulate container door stuffing.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Metric Calculation (cm to CBM):

    CBM per Carton = (Length (cm) × Width (cm) × Height (cm)) / 1,000,000
Total CBM = CBM per Carton × Quantity
Total Cubic Feet (CFT) = Total CBM × 35.3147

    Imperial Calculation (Inches to CBM):

    CBM per Carton = (Length (in) × Width (in) × Height (in)) / 61,023.7
Total CFT = (Length (in) × Width (in) × Height (in) × Qty) / 1,728

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why does actual container capacity differ from nominal CBM?

    Nominal CBM is the theoretical interior volume of an empty container. In real-world cargo loading, cartons rarely tessellate perfectly against corrugated container walls, leaving 10% to 15% void space ("broken stowage").

    How does CBM affect freight charges in LCL shipping?

    Ocean LCL cargo is billed on a "Weight or Measurement" (W/M) basis where 1 CBM is equivalent to 1,000 kg (1 metric ton). Whichever yields the higher revenue ton is used as the billable basis.

    Can I mix different box sizes in this calculator?

    Yes. Click "+ Add Another Item" to enter as many different carton shapes, sizes, and quantities as needed. The calculator computes individual row subtotals and aggregates them into the grand total.

    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Sourdough Hydration Calculator eliminates one of the most common pitfalls in artisan bread baking: confusing bowl hydration (added water divided by added flour) with true total hydration. Because active sourdough starters and levains contain substantial amounts of water and flour (typically 50% water and 50% flour by weight), neglecting the starter's contribution skews your hydration calculations by 2% to 5%, turning what you thought was a manageable 68% dough into a sticky 73% dough.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Starter Split & True Hydration Formulas:

    Starter Flour Weight = Starter Weight / (1 + (Starter Hydration% / 100))
Starter Water Weight = Starter Weight - Starter Flour Weight
Total Batch Flour = Total Added Flour + Starter Flour
Total Batch Water = Total Added Water + Starter Water
True Total Hydration % = (Total Batch Water / Total Batch Flour) × 100
Inoculation % = (Starter Weight / Total Added Flour) × 100
Salt % = (Salt Weight / Total Batch Flour) × 100

    Technical Specifications & Mechanics

    Calculations evaluate starter hydration dynamically. If a baker maintains a stiff levain at $60\%$ hydration, the tool correctly derives $62.5\text{g}$ flour and $37.5\text{g}$ water for every $100\text{g}$ of starter. Numerical conversions between grams and ounces preserve decimal accuracy ($1\text{ oz} = 28.34952\text{ g}$).

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    Flour absorbency varies based on ash content, milling fineness, and ambient humidity. When baking with new flour brands, hold back 5% of the recipe's water ("bassi" technique) during initial autolyse and add it back incrementally during salt incorporation.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why does my high-hydration sourdough dough flatten out when turned out of the banneton?

    Dough spreading ("pancaking") indicates weak gluten structure, insufficient dough tension during pre-shaping, or over-proofing during bulk fermentation. Reduce hydration by 3% to 5% until handling technique is perfected.

    Does salt percentage affect sourdough fermentation speed?

    Yes. Salt tightens the gluten network and regulates yeast activity. Standard salt levels range from 1.8% to 2.2%. Dropping below 1.5% accelerates fermentation but risks weak dough elasticity.

    Can I count milk or beer as water in this calculator?

    Cow's milk is roughly 88% water and 12% fats/solids; beer is roughly 95% water. When replacing water with milk, multiply milk weight by 0.88 to determine its effective water contribution.

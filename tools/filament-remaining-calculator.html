    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Filament Remaining Calculator prevents failed prints and midnight runout disasters. One of the most frustrating experiences in 3D printing is starting an 8-hour print with an unknown quantity of leftover filament, only to find the printer dry-printing air at 90% completion.

    Because empty spools weigh between 140g and 250g depending on brand and material (cardboard vs. plastic), placing a partial spool on a kitchen scale gives a misleading gross weight. This tool automatically subtracts the known brand tare weight to give you exact remaining net grams, equivalent length in meters, and an instant "Can I Print This?" check against your slicer's requirements.

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    1. Choose Measurement Mode:
      • Mode 1 (Scale Weight): Place your partial spool on any digital kitchen scale. Select your spool brand from the preset list (Bambu, Polymaker, eSUN, Prusament, Hatchbox) and enter the gross weight.
      • Mode 2 (Ruler Measurement): If you do not have a scale, measure the inner hub diameter, outer filament edge, and spool width in millimeters.
    2. Select Material Density & Diameter: Choose your filament type (PLA, PETG, ABS, TPU) and diameter (1.75mm vs 2.85mm).
    3. Input Slicer Required Grams: Enter the grams needed from your slicer preview (e.g., Bambu Studio or PrusaSlicer).
    4. Review Pre-Flight Status: If the status card is green, you have enough filament with a comfortable buffer. If red, do not start without loading an AMS backup spool.
    5. Copy Summary: Copy the formatted spool log or click Send to Cost Calculator to price your finished model.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Filament remaining calculations apply exact volumetric physics:

    • Scale Tare Deduction ($W_{\text{net}}$):
      $$W_{\text{net}} = \max(0, W_{\text{gross on scale}} - W_{\text{empty tare}})$$
    • Cross-Sectional Area ($A_{\text{filament}}$):
      $$r_{\text{cm}} = \frac{d_{\text{mm}}}{20}$$ $$A = \pi \times r_{\text{cm}}^2$$ (For 1.75mm filament: $A \approx 0.02405\text{ cm}^2$; for 2.85mm filament: $A \approx 0.06379\text{ cm}^2$).
    • Remaining Length in Meters ($L_{\text{meters}}$):
      $$V_{\text{cm}^3} = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{\rho_{\text{density}}}$$ $$L_{\text{meters}} = \frac{V_{\text{cm}^3}}{A \times 100}$$
    • Ruler Spool Volume with Packing Factor ($V_{\text{ruler}}$):
      $$V_{\text{mm}^3} = \pi \times \left( \left(\frac{D_{\text{out}}}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{D_{\text{hub}}}{2}\right)^2 \right) \times W_{\text{spool}} \times 0.78$$ $$W_{\text{net}} = \frac{V_{\text{mm}^3}}{1000} \times \rho_{\text{density}}$$

    Realistic Example & Expected Output

    Scenario: You place a partial Bambu Lab Cardboard spool of PLA on your kitchen scale and it reads 465g. Your slicer says the model requires 190g.

    • Gross Weight: 465 g
    • Empty Tare Preset: 215 g
    • Remaining Net Weight: $465 - 215 = 250\text{ g}$
    • Remaining Length: $\frac{250\text{ g}}{1.24\text{ g/cm}^3 \times 0.02405\text{ cm}^2 \times 100} = 83.8\text{ meters}$
    • Slicer Pre-Flight Check: $250\text{g} - 190\text{g} = +60\text{g}$ spare.
    • Result: Green "Yes, enough filament!" with comfortable safety buffer.

    Limitations & Browser Boundaries

    Cardboard spools can absorb ambient atmospheric moisture, occasionally increasing tare weight by 5g to 10g in humid environments. Plastic injection-molded spools have tighter tolerances (±3g). Keep a 10g buffer for safety.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What if my filament runout sensor pauses the printer automatically?

    While modern runout sensors pause printing when the filament tail trips the switch, restarting often leaves a visible layer line or seam blemish on cosmetic models. Calculating in advance avoids interrupted prints.

    How much does filament moisture affect the scale weight?

    Even heavily saturated hygroscopic filaments (like Nylon or PETG) typically absorb no more than 1% to 2% moisture by weight (approx 10g per 1kg). Moisture has a negligible impact on remaining weight calculations.

    Can I weigh the spool while it is mounted on the printer?

    No. Spool holder friction and extruder PTFE tube tension pull against digital scales, creating inaccurate readings. Always take the spool off the holder to weigh it freely on a flat table.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Coffee extraction ratios define the mass ratio of dry ground coffee ($C$) to brewing water ($W$):

    • Brew Ratio ($R$):
      $$R = \frac{W_{\text{grams}}}{C_{\text{grams}}}$$ (e.g., $320\text{g water} / 20\text{g coffee} = 16 \implies \text{Ratio 1:16}$)
    • Water from Known Coffee Mass:
      $$W = C \times R$$
    • Coffee from Known Water Volume:
      $$C = \frac{W}{R}$$
    • Bloom Pour Mass ($W_{\text{bloom}}$):
      $$W_{\text{bloom}} = C \times 2.5$$ (2.5 times dry coffee mass provides thorough wetting without premature drip-through).
    • First Main Pour ($W_{\text{pour 1}}$):
      $$W_{\text{pour 1}} = W_{\text{total}} \times 0.60$$

    • Water Mass Equivalence: $1.00\text{ gram} = 1.00\text{ milliliter}$ of liquid water at standard brewing temperatures.
    • Volume Approximations: Level tablespoon conversions use $7.0\text{g}$ per level tbsp of roasted whole beans and $5.0\text{g}$ per level tbsp of medium ground coffee.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Should I weigh my coffee as whole beans or after grinding?

    Always weigh your whole coffee beans on your digital scale before grinding, then grind the exact dose. High-quality single-dose grinders have minimal retention (<0.2g), ensuring your ground weight matches your calculated recipe.

    Why is water weight on the scale different from the brewed coffee in my cup?

    Dry coffee grounds absorb approximately twice their weight in water during brewing (called water retention). If you pour 320g of water over 20g of grounds, the grounds will retain ~40g of water, resulting in ~280g of liquid beverage in your mug. Coffee recipes are always calculated using the total water poured.

    What water temperature is best for light vs. dark roasts?

    Light roasts are denser and extract more slowly; brew them with near-boiling water (94°C–96°C / 201°F–205°F) to extract sweet fruit notes. Dark roasts are more porous and extract rapidly; use cooler water (88°C–91°C / 190°F–196°F) to prevent unpleasant ash and bitterness.

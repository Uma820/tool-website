    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Aquarium Volume Calculator determines both the nominal outer volume and the true net water capacity of any home aquarium. Commercial fish tanks are marketed by their nominal glass dimensions (e.g. "20 Gallon High"), but in reality, glass thickness, top waterline headspace, substrate gravel, rock hardscape, and driftwood significantly reduce the amount of water the aquarium holds—often by 15% to 25%. Knowing your exact net water volume prevents fatal chemical overdoses when adding water conditioners or medications, prevents buying undersized heaters, and gives you the exact floor weight load for stand safety.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Volume calculations depend on the 3D geometric cross-section:

    • Rectangular Tank: $$\text{Gross Volume (cu in)} = \text{Length} \times \text{Width} \times \text{Height}$$
    • Bowfront Tank: $$\text{Footprint Area} = (\text{Length} \times \text{Side Width}) + \left(\frac{2}{3} \times \text{Length} \times (\text{Center Depth} - \text{Side Width})\right)$$
    • Cylinder Tank: $$\text{Gross Volume} = \pi \times \left(\frac{\text{Diameter}}{2}\right)^2 \times \text{Height}$$
    • Hexagonal Tank: $$\text{Footprint Area} = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} \times \text{Side Length}^2$$
    • Corner Quarter-Cylinder: $$\text{Footprint Area} = \frac{\pi \times \text{Radius}^2}{4}$$
    • Unit Conversions: $$\text{US Gallons} = \frac{\text{Cubic Inches}}{231}$$ $$\text{Liters} = \text{US Gallons} \times 3.78541$$
    • Net Water Volume: $$\text{Net Volume} = \text{Internal Volume (accounting for glass \& headspace)} \times (1 - \text{Displacement \%})$$

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I calculate bowfront aquarium volume accurately?

    A bowfront tank consists of a rectangular base plus a curved front segment. Our calculator computes the rectangular area ($L \times W_{\text{side}}$) and adds the parabolic bow curve area ($\frac{2}{3} \times L \times (W_{\text{center}} - W_{\text{side}})$), then multiplies by height to get the true three-dimensional volume.

    Will my floor support a 55-gallon or 75-gallon aquarium?

    A 55-gallon tank weighs around 620 lbs, and a 75-gallon tank weighs around 850 lbs. In modern residential construction, placing the tank perpendicular to floor joists against a load-bearing wall is generally safe up to 75 gallons. For 100+ gallon tanks (weighing over 1,200 lbs), consulting a structural engineer or adding basement floor jacks is recommended.

    How many pounds of gravel do I need per gallon?

    The standard general rule is 1 to 1.5 pounds of gravel per gallon of tank capacity for a 1.5 to 2-inch bed. However, shallow tanks with large footprints (like 40 Gallon Breeders) require more gravel than tall tanks with small footprints. Our tool calculates gravel based on exact bottom surface area for greater precision.

    What This Tool Does & Problem It Solves

    The Battery Runtime Calculator calculates accurate backup duration for home inverters, solar energy storage systems, camper van batteries, and uninterruptible power supplies (UPS).

    Most homeowners and solar installers mistakenly divide nominal battery watt-hours by connected load without accounting for two critical electrical factors: Depth of Discharge (DoD) and Inverter Inefficiency. Draining a lead-acid battery completely to 0% destroys its lead plates and voids manufacturer warranties within months. By applying safe DoD thresholds (50% for flooded tubular, 60% for AGM, 85% for LiFePO4) and inverter conversion losses (typically 12% to 15%), this tool provides realistic, safe backup estimates.

    6. Review Runtime & Reference Table: Read your exact runtime in hours and minutes, and check the load benchmark table to see how turning off non-essential appliances extends your backup.

    Mathematical Formulas Used

    Battery runtime calculations follow rigorous electrical physics:

    • Total Stored Energy ($E_{\text{total}}$):
      $$E_{\text{total (Wh)}} = V_{\text{bank}} \times \text{Capacity}_{\text{Ah}}$$
    • Usable Battery Energy ($E_{\text{usable}}$):
      $$E_{\text{usable (Wh)}} = E_{\text{total (Wh)}} \times \left( \frac{\text{DoD \%}}{100} \right)$$
    • Effective Power Draw ($P_{\text{battery}}$):
      $$P_{\text{battery (Watts)}} = \frac{P_{\text{appliance load}}}{\eta_{\text{inverter efficiency}}}$$
    • Runtime Hours ($T_{\text{hours}}$):
      $$T_{\text{hours}} = \frac{E_{\text{usable (Wh)}}}{P_{\text{battery (Watts)}}}$$

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why shouldn't I discharge my lead-acid battery to 100%?

    Deeply discharging lead-acid batteries below 50% causes hard lead sulfate crystals to permanently harden onto the plates (sulfation), irreversibly destroying capacity and reducing battery lifespan from 4 years down to under 6 months.

    Can I mix old and new batteries in the same bank?

    No. Never mix old and new batteries, or different brands and capacities. The older, weaker battery will pull down the charging voltage of the new battery, causing chronic undercharging and premature failure.

    How do I convert Amps to Watts?

    Multiply Amps by the System Voltage ($\text{Watts} = \text{Volts} \times \text{Amps}$). For example, a 25 Amp draw on a 12V battery equals $12 \times 25 = 300\text{ Watts}$.
